Beings Diverse and Malign Two copies of this bestiary exist, both in the town of Crossroads. One is in the shielded reading room (advanced section) of the spiral tower of the Octocirculi Academy. The other is in the private vaults of the Guardians’ Guild. Each bestiary is a large, locked, brass-bound volume chained to a lectern; access is available on request (and possibly after payment) to those deemed suitable, stable, and discreet. The information outlined is not only not generally known but not safe to know. To speak of, or even hint at, some of the things revealed is to invite the attention of the most unwelcome kind. Caveat lector! I, Signoret, sage and wizard, pupil of the late Hawklord, and student of the Octocirculi Academy, being by virtue of personal experience well informed where the more extraordinary inhabitants of this world are concerned, have decided to compile a bestiary. In so doing I have been moved to create as accurate an account as possible and thus I go beyond my own knowledge and quote freely from sources I know to be reliable. Herein are compiled the travelers’ tales of Nathal and Virgil, the wisdom of the Duchess Sibyl, and the contents of numerous grimoires, scrolls, and librams. I have resorted to reading from an occult tome that describes the city of Phantos; so appalling are the secrets therein evealed that I quote them verbatim, lest some reader accuse me of inventing its horrors. So too have I studied the writings of witches both light and malign; I have trawled through accursed works that give a seeker knowledge — a terrible knowledge I would sometimes rather forget. Be clear that I have no fondness for the dark things I describe; and yet I offer what I have read of the motives and goals of such terrible sorcerous entities as Lilith and Baphomet without comment — let the reader ascertain the truth of their claims or prove them a lie. Grim indeed are the threats that face us, and if one should wonder why any sane man stays in these lands, then let him remember that not all is given over to horror — there are good folk and good places aplenty here in the realms of Ainerêve. The Guardians’ Guild is one beacon of light against the darkness, but all kingdoms cry out for more heroes and heroines. Will you be of their number? Lest the reader should balk at the sheer number and variety of beasts, let us recall the gates leading to worlds far and near, to places, times, and dimensions as diverse as the universe itself. Every kind of creature comes crawling, slithering, loping, or charging through the gates at Crossroads — truly the gates are both blessing and curse. To better categorize and explain these beings I have applied a system of numerical gradation and probability mapping; the discerning adventurer will see from the number ranges that the danger of each creature, its resistances and abilities, are all clearly expressed. Now, on to a consideration of the beings an adventurer passing through Crossroads or crossing the lands of Ainerêve might meet. Gird your sword, ready your shield; if magic be your trade, then prepare a spell — for surely battle is upon you and the difference betwixt life and death no more than a quick wit or a quicker blade.

They are found in unhallowed graveyards and defiled temples where the priesthood and acolytes were murdered by some rival, evil god’s minions. Abilities and Powers: The accursed are undead creatures devoid of will and without any ability to speak of, other than inflicting harm. They are immune to curses, mental affliction effects, and other necromantic energies that would seek to harm or control them. Each accursed also possesses a rotting power. If this d8 attack penetrates a target’s chosen active defense, it causes d4 to be lost from the Passive Defense Pool (DP) each round until a revitalization effect is used to stop it. Weapons, Armor, and Treasure: The accursed do not generally wear armor; if one does, it’s falling apart and basically useless. They cannot wield any weapon that provides specific bonuses (being minor), but they can be described as wielding whatever the GM describes (mechanically, it makes no difference). They carry only trinkets: 1–6 coins and 1% chance of 1 minor magic item per group for treasure.

Ambulant Thresher Type: Standard (plant) Threat Dice: Natural 2d10 Extra Attacks: d10 (suffering slumber attack) Hit Points: 40 (30/10) [fast, large, magical; ×2] Damage Reduction: 4 (natural) Saving Throw: d10 Battle Phase d6 Legends and Lore: Three summers past, the druid Cathbad, when returning from some far distant sphere, brought back with him three strange seedlings. These plants, each with oddly tentacle-like leaves, grew swiftly into life-threatening man-eaters and multiplied rapidly. They now rove woodlands seeking prey, and if unable to get it, will quest even into villages or farms. According to Nathal, who has hunted down a score of these horrors, when fully grown a thresher resembles a bulbous black cactus crowned with a quartet of long purple tentacles. One can grow to twice the size of a man. Its movement is a wobbling undulation of its lower quarters that lets it ripple across the ground with surprising swiftness. Why the threshers seek out sentient prey is unknown; however, in the Grimoire of Giseld, it is written that there are unsettled realms where thoughts are food and where pain-wracked consciousness can be channeled to alien gods for whom suffering is sustenance. The plant-beings were first known in Crossroads, although they have spread across the land despite the efforts of Nathal. Abilities and Powers: A thresher can strike at multiple foes with its tentacles; range equals its trunk’s height — typically 5 to 12 feet. Strikes penetrating to reduce Passive DP cause pain and lethargy, so that those struck multiple times will be slowed (save vs. Fortitude) and eventually slip into a suffering slumber where they dream of torture and death. Simultaneously, the thresher digs small roots into their brains to drain nutrients and in time extinguish life. The spiky projections on a thresher’s trunk pose a hazard to one fighting it at close quarters. Weapons, Armor, and Treasure: The cactus-like trunk of the thresher is equal to hide armor; spikes may strike an adversary in close melee (+1 to potential harm when engaged unarmed or with short weapons). They have no treasure, but legend has it that eating the plant flesh of the thresher grants one a glimpse of the final memories of its recent victims. Afriti Type: Exceptional Threat Dice: Arcane 3d12 ~ Natural d6 ~ Melee d4 ~ Ranged d4 Hit Points: 72 (54/18) [fast, medium, preternatural; ×2] Damage Reduction: Immune to fire, normal or magical Saving Throw: d12 or 3d12 versus Elementalism Fire spells (other than Harm) Battle Phase d16

Legends and Lore: There are combustible realms in Ainerêve where fury flashes ignite into raging elemental storms; such are elemental spheres of fire and magma. The afriti, it is said, are but one of many fire-beings that dwell in such inhospitable realms, although the gnome explorer Sprouta claims they have fashioned a city of sorts that floats upon an ever-burning sea of flames. Sparks of anger form the afriti; fire and smoke bound into a shifting, sometimes man-like, form. When they attack, their upper body blazes outwards in a fan-like glare in which a hateful countenance can be seen. As to their will and purposes, who can say? But one summoned into our own realms is a thing of spite and fury; if you meet one, lay it low, for its anger cannot be quenched, not even in the depths of the sea. Abilities and Powers: The afriti are invulnerable to fire; however, an attack, magical or otherwise, that incorporates water or ice will cause double normal harm. The afriti creates a field of fire about itself that burns anyone close enough to strike a blow against it. At will, it can project fire up to twenty feet away: such a strike ignites combustible materials and causes ongoing burns. It can envelop a foe in its body of magma and flame, immolating its victim within its fiery core. He who binds an afriti can force it to do three tasks; the greater amongst their number can cause reality shifts sufficient to grant a wish, but not one that is overly powerful or that disturbs the stability of a given realm too much. Let summoner or binder beware: all such wishes and tasks are fuelled by hate; that which comes forth from anger is ignobly born, and surely any creation of hate will bear bitter fruit. Weapons, Armor, and Treasure: For occult reasons, brass does not melt in the hands of an afriti; they have an affinity for it, adorn themselves in curious brazen jewelry, and sometimes bear other artifacts of brass — likewise, fire opals, bloodstones, and rubies are unharmed by an afriti’s fiery touch. Treasure carried is in the range of a trove: 200–2000 coins and a 30% chance of d3 magic items. Amphisbaena Type: Exceptional Threat Dice: Arcane 3d8 ~ Natural 3d6 Hit Points: 72 (18/54) [tough, huge, preternatural; ×3] Damage Reduction: Immune to mental and spiritual afflictions Saving Throw: d10 Battle Phase d4 Legends and Lore: Ouroboros unbound; cyclicity become chaos; in essence, a large snake with a head at each end of its broad barrel-like body with mouths big enough to swallow a horse whole. Around the amphisbaena, vegetation spoils and dies. Only in water does it regain tranquility. If submerged beneath the water, one head swallows the other, and Ouroboros is reborn. From whence does it come? From dreams of greed, I am told, that cause Ouroboros to unbind; the natural order is usurped by lust for something — usually wealth or power. That which should be tranquil and peaceful, wise and eternal, becomes a raging coiling avatar of death. Abilities and Powers: One of the cavernous serpentine maws can breathe poisonous vapors; the other has fangs dripping a dreadful green caustic venom that pits the very ground it falls upon. Weapons, Armor, and Treasure: True to the greed that gave it birth, the amphisbaena always seeks, or possessively guards, some object of desire — it will be a thing of great value, whether financial, magical, or living: a person, an artist, a beauty, a creator or an inventor may be taken this way. A traveler from Meath, one whose beard was green, hinted to me that at times the very victim of the amphisbaena is its creator, although how or why that should be is a mystery upon which I cannot comment. Angel Type: Legendary Threat Dice: Melee 2d12+8 ~ Ranged 2d10+5 ~ Natural 2d8+7 ~ Arcane d12+5 Hit Points: 60 (30/30) [normal, nedium, supernatural; ×2.5] Damage Reduction: By armor, shield, or magic item. Saving Throw: 2d10 Battle Phase d10 or d12 Legends and Lore: Angels are beatific celestial beings, as one would probably imagine. There’s not some imperative that each has to have a pair of wings, much less a harp, but all will be powerful, not only in might but also in wisdom. Angels are symbolic of all that is just and holy in the spiritual world. They often serve as proxies to the celestial gods. Their incarnation in the mortal realm is usually humanoid and of the most handsome and superior sort but depends on its associated deity and culture. A high-ranking priest may summon one, or the master deity may send an archangel on a mission to the mortal realm. They are proud and willful, rarely merciful if provoked, and do not take direct commands unless issued by a higher power. An angel does not ordinarily stay around once its mission is finished unless it must guard some important, holy place. Archangels are supposed to exist, few in number but even more powerful: the radiance of their glory is said to burn up mere mortals who gaze on them. Abilities and Powers: An angel’s average ability is at a d12 (basic) + d12 (specialty). Angels typically have adept, Theurgist, and warrior advantages and theurgist feats; but just about any skill imaginable is possible. Languages spoken include the common trade language, plus Celestial. Also, angels can invoke the following powers using their Arcane threat dice: Spiritual blast (Challenge Die d8, Save None): A harmful stream of brilliant, electric-blue light that sears the target’s soul, bypassing Agility DP. Add +4 to Challenge Die to make it an area effect spell. Command the infidel (Challenge Die d10, Save Fortitude → Willpower): The angel intones a command, and the target cannot help but obey. Weapons, Armor, and Treasure: Any weapon and any armor, usually magical with up to +5 in bonuses to protection or damage. Their usually ranged weapon is a bow. Angels may have any piece of equipment desired but do not carry food, coin, or other sundries because they don’t have bodily needs. They will carry a magic item other than their weapons 85% of the time — usually an orb or wand of some kind. Typically, angels carry a special holy weapon (any type) with the following properties: Holy Weapon: The general properties of the weapon match that of the type. It has up to a +5 bonus to damage. It can also do +8 non-physical Energy harm once a round (this is a permanent effect). In the hands of an angel, a holy weapon does triple damage versus demons (including the bonus and non-physical harm). Anthropophagi Type: Standard Threat Dice: Natural 2d6 ~ Melee d4 Hit Points: 18 (9/9) [normal, medium, magical; ×1.5] Damage Reduction: –2 versus natural or bludgeoning attacks Saving Throw: d4 to d8, immune to mental affliction Battle Phase d6 Legends and Lore: My source for information about these man-like cannibals may not be reliable. Much of what I am told comes from the goblinoid half-orc Throk. He tells me he has seen the dreaded Revel of Flesh, a dance and unholy feasting conjured up from the darkest pits. He saw the anthropophagi, their near-naked bodies deformed by the spirit-energy of the victims they eat — frightened faces bulge from bellies, extra eyes open on arms, a second face on the back of the head, writhing mouths opening like wounds on torso and limb. Among them are the most fearsome of their kind, the blemmyes, so deformed as to now resemble little more than a headless torso of combined flesh, a trunk from which sprout arms and legs at awkward angles, askew, misaligned. A great face forms in the midst of the torso, its thick-lipped mouth full of teeth and ready to devour yet more men. The Revel of Flesh is spreading, I am told; it is a dance writhing in the shadows and at night the anthropophagi stumble, bumble, and tumble into moonlit groves to begin a hunt for more eyes and mouths to join their horrid feasting. Abilities and Powers: Attacks are a mixture of bites and clubbing strikes; if a victim is grappled, he will be bitten many times. A bite from a blemmyes will induce a blasphemous craving in a victim, an unholy desire to dance and feast on the flesh of men, or worse still, a desire to be eaten, a wish to be consumed (save vs. Fortitude → Willpower, once at the time of the bite, then once per day). Weapons, Armor, and Treasure: Incidental wealth may abound; anthropophagi have no use for a victim’s possessions — they will be scattered, as may be entrails and body parts, in the wake of their dreadful dance. It is possible that within one of these cannibals, an item of value might be found, eaten thoughtlessly as its struggling owner was devoured. Arimaspians Type: Standard; some exceptional Threat Dice: Natural d6 ~ Arcane 2d6 ~ Melee d10 ~ Ranged d8 Hit Points: 30 (15/15) [normal, medium, magical; ×1.5] Damage Reduction: Immune to Elementalism (Air) spells Saving Throw: 2d10 Battle Phase d10 (+3 reaction modifier for d8 Agility) Legends and Lore: Of the arimaspians, I speak with authority, for I was a “guest” of their Duke Pathafarial in his cloud-borne palace. A single eye glares from the forehead of these cyclops-like human-sized warriors. They are ruthless reavers, plunderers of gold — they are adorned with gold jewelry and lust after the metal. Dressed in flamboyant nautical garments, they have a bronze, almost gold, hue to their skin and an evil demeanor. They enslave griffins and are oft encountered mounted upon or leading such beasts. They are pirates of the skies, flying upon their fearsome subjugated steeds. Abilities and Powers: They can conjure forth mists and winds, and the greatest amongst them can cause clouds to become as ice and bring forth sleet or snow — among their own kind, these wielders of elemental magic are called navigators. An ice cloud formed as a flying creature passes through it becomes a prison (Challenge Die d10; save vs. Fortitude). The ice clouds stay aloft unless cast to earth by the arimaspian sorcerer. Their warriors, invariably led by a flamboyant or aggressive captain, wield cutlasses, and some bear fearsome weapons of metal and wood that can shoot balls of lead into a man’s heart. These somehow make use of an alchemical black powder. Only the arimaspians and the sea elves know the secrets of such “pistols,” as they name them. Flying ships have been reported, upon which are mounted siege engines of iron and flame, so-called “cannons,” able, they say, to lay low a city from the air. Weapons, Armor, and Treasure: Gold, jewels, enchanted weapons, magical items that grant the power of flight, even flying ships, islands, and palaces, all await the adventuring band that can defeat the arimaspians. Their cutlasses add bonus threat points to Melee attacks with microbursts of air at every point of impact (+1 threat point per strike). Some also wield bizarre weapons that belch noise, fire, and brimstony smoke, as below. Alchemical black powder “pistol.” Roll Ranged ability against Challenge Die d4 to hit; a 12 bypasses all armor. Threat points equal Prowess → Agility → Ranged Threat. Banshee Type: Legendary Threat Dice: Arcane 3d14 ~ Melee 2d4 ~ Natural d6+4 ~ Ranged d4 Hit Points: 105 (53/52) [normal, medium, supernatural; ×2.5] Damage Reduction: –2 against all potential harm Saving Throw: d12, or 2d14 versus spiritual afflictions Battle Phase d10 Legends and Lore: Fearsome spirit-women of Maedoen, the banshee are harbingers of death. Oft appearing as old crones holding out bloodied clothing, with sly grins upon their age-wracked faces; it is said that a warrior recognizing his own garb in the banshee’s claws is doomed. Alternatively, the banshee might take the guise of a fair maid to lead a warrior astray — to where something horrid lies in wait. In the library of Duchess Sibyl, I found a scroll reportedly penned by the Eldritch Man. It says the banshee seek vengeance for every bitter and heartless deed warrior-men have done and that they shall take vengeance until moon and sun shine together in a darkened sky. Abilities and Powers: One who sees his own clothes in the banshee’s grasp, hears her mournful and frightsome keening wail, or is led away by her from his comrades into the dark wood, mist, or mire, falls under a curse, and death will stalk him by day and by night. (Save versus Willpower, or else suffer +2 potential harm from all traps and hazards, plus –1 rank per tier on all Saving Throws until the curse is lifted.) Weapons, Armor, and Treasure: Some locations, the sites of remorseless deeds perhaps, become nexial gathering places for the banshee, and in such locales, the bodies of many warriors might lie, adorned still with armor and treasures, with sword and steel. Baphomet Type: Legendary (avatar) Threat Dice: Arcane 3d12 ~ Melee 3d12 ~ Ranged 2d12~ Natural 2d10 Hit Points: 108 (81/27) [fast, large, supernatural; ×3] Damage Reduction: d12 (plate armor) Saving Throw: 3d14 Battle Phase d14 Legends and Lore: Here I shall write of that which men call Baphomet; I find little, even in the darkest grimoires, to tell me the truth of this being that some claim is demonic, but for certain he has truck with much that is demoniacal and of the shadow. There are the oddest hints, webs of thought linking Baphomet with the Templar Knights, but how can this be? They that so staunchly, even heartlessly, cleanse (or persecute?) all they deem heretics, witches, or demonists, let alone demons, how can they have any communion with the Sabbatic Goat? Baphomet can put on the guise of any man or spirit — why, some scrolls even claim any god — that he chooses. The avatar so assumed can take many forms, but when contemplating Baphomet, the reader of this bestiary is best served by considering Baphomet’s accepted image — half male, half female, the body parts of human and goat, phallic in its strength, creative in its potency; an embodiment of occult and alchemical concepts and power. To the inner cabal of those who secretly worship Baphomet, his existence is known to be the result of all humanity’s desires, all magical workings, the wizardly attempts to lift humanity’s status of the divine. To the true Baphomet believer, there are no real gods, no true devils — both are manifestations of limitation; artificialities created by human consciousness fearing its own power; thus, they say, men have made two masters, two rods for our backs. And yet, their texts say the power of freedom will not be denied, so we have fashioned an occult key in addition to our prison. That key is Baphomet, and both Heaven and Hell fear the nullification Baphomet will bring: death, night, and a new dawn in a new Eden — one built by the true will of man. Baphomet hates the powers of Hell and all so-called gods, whether divine or darkly satanic: they are artificialities that existed before him (or so he and his adherents claim) and obstacles to his will. He would undo the whole of creation, turn it back to primal matter, so that man can start again and build a better and more fitting world — or so Baphomet believes. All gods are demiurges, craftsmen of inferior skill who have built a prison for life rather than paradise to their followers. In this view, Baphomet is “good” because he will liberate life by destroying the illusion/matter that binds us. Good and Evil both are part of the system Baphomet would destroy. And thus it is that Baphomet seeks some method to undo creation here in Ainerêve; to reshape Earth via humanity’s dreams — to reorder reality in reverse from the dream world back into the physical. To do this, he must gain power here, and he plays a long game, willing to take centuries, for what are years when placed against eternity? Heaven and Hell have become aware of his strategies. They have woven a web of energy, light, and dark across Ainerêve to bind Baphomet’s power as best they may go through a strange and unexpected alliance. In response, Lilith, Baphomet’s bride — and some say creatrix — has sent her presence too, seeking a way to free her consort’s full energies once more. Abilities and Powers: Although many forms might be taken, I have read of three (all extraordinary mortals): the knight, the bard, and the emissary. The knight brings war, is garbed in impenetrable steel, is fear made manifest, and can stand in tourney against any champion. The bard would appear to bring merriment, and jovial clowns may follow in his wake; quick of wit and sharp eye, he makes a play of every drama he sees and stirs drama where peace once prevailed. Quick to feel boredom and carelessness of those he might hurt, he moves on swiftly after spreading his stage across a tavern, village, or even a kingdom. The emissary he offers much, is peace made real; soft, kind, cheerful, quick to please others, and ever wanting the approval of those he meets, and yet, if it is not given, he will fall into the depths of sorrow, and swiftly thereafter fade beneath the shadow, and in his going call forth bard or knight — or something even more fearsome, bestial, and dark. Weapons, Armor, and Treasure: Any. Without exception, Baphomet’s avatars will be adorned with strange, often cursed and dangerous, items of the most complex sorcery. Baphomet may choose to leave such behind when seemingly defeated; indeed, his whole purpose for coming might be to leave a trinket, an item worthy of Pandora, when he departs. Treasure possessed is a hoard: 1000–10,000 coins but 100% chance of 4 magic items. Basilisk Type: Standard to Exceptional Threat Dice: Natural 2d10 to 3d10 ~ Ranged (special) ~ Arcane (special) Extra Attacks: Killing miasma, gaze, and rotting bite Hit Points: 60 to 90 (15/45 to 23/67) [tough, huge, preternatural; ×3) Damage Reduction: +2 hit points (tough hide) Saving Throw: d10 to d12 Battle Phase d6 Legends and Lore: Existing in multiple forms, or perhaps breeds, the basilisk is the king of serpents, distinguished by a crown-like crest and a single white spot between its narrow reptilian eyes — unlike other serpents, its eyes are man-like, with heavily hooded lids and a pronounced slant. In youth no more than twelve fingers in length, the thing grows both in size and danger, so that when fully grown, it rears up to the height of a man atop a horse, and in length exceeds that of three horses. Abilities and Powers: Greater basilisks exude a killing miasma, so their very presence causes death, not only in man but in beast and plant too; where goes the basilisk, there goes death and decay and desert. Their glance is fatal, stopping breath; their bite so caustic that the venom penetrates not only the victim but also anything or anyone touching the victim. A basilisk biting a horse will kill its rider too, the very skin and flesh rotting from man and mount, leaving nothing but bones and skulls, half-clothed in the flesh. Killing miasma: Any creature within 5 feet must save versus Fortitude or fall incapacitated and take d10 to 3d10 harm every round thereafter, depending on the basilisk’s size and age. Gaze: Victim must roll vs. Competence → Perception to avert gaze else suffer harm directly to Passive Defense (d10 to 3d10, depending on the basilisk’s size and age). Rotting bite: Regular shields and armor can protect the defender but make item saving throws versus the basilisk’s threat rank dice or be destroyed. Make a Fortitude save if even one point reduces Passive DP; failure to save inflicts another threat dice roll affecting Passive DP. Weapons, Armor, and Treasure: I am told by the elven traveler Nathal that a visitor to the Seelie Court brought a tale told in the east, that each basilisk guards a gem of great size (value 10,000–60,000 in coin), and that if this gem is properly incubated, a dragon will come forth from it. Of the truth of this, I cannot say. Brownie Type: Minor to Standard Threat Dice: Any attack type, d4 to 2d4 Hit Points: 4 to 8 (2/2 to 4/4) [normal, tiny, magical; ×1] Damage Reduction: –1 if wearing properly sized brownie armor Saving Throw: d4 to d8 Battle Phase d4 to d8 Legends and Lore: The brownie is a tiny shee that dwells unseen in the homes of mortals. Abilities and Powers: Brownies have magic of a kind sort that helps with fixing, hiding, and bringing luck. Weapons, Armor, and Treasure: It is a common error of all-too-greedy men to mistake the brownie for some other type of shee altogether and seek from it some fabled and non-existent pot of gold as if the thing were a miserly leprechaun — doing so is unwise. Foolhardy are the adventurers of every world, every realm, and as those passing through Crossroads are no different, I will include in this bestiary a description of those fabled beasts called dragons. But first, a word to the wise, a suggestion to every would-be dragon hunter: don’t! Do not seek out the great wyrms, go not near their cavernous abodes, pretend for all the world that they do not exist, and hope beyond hope that one never decides to seek out you. Now, as this advice will doubtless be ignored, we move on to the creatures themselves, although truth be told, they deserve their own volume. When met in the flesh (or in the scale?), a dragon is much as traditionally depicted — a fiery, flying, scaled monstrosity; its wings are bat-like, its body serpentine. They have varying limbs, hues, and abilities: four-legged, fire-breathing specimens are the most widely reported. And yet, be prepared for more snake-like horrors, with but two forepaws, or six-legged examples that will sweep down upon you, blast you to the floor with their wings, and then snatch you up with your companions, fly high, and drop you. Some dragons are tied to a single element. Thus there are storm dragons of the air, water dragons of the deep, rock-like dragons, and even lava dragons. Cultural influences too shape the dragon and its powers; thus, I am told, in certain oriental realms, the dragons’ harbor wisdom rather than hatred — a manifestation of cultural expectation, not of the act that created the creature. Legends and Lore: I have many reports, and yet I must be concise. Perhaps a consideration of what forms most dragons is in order. Dragons are born in unsettled lands; they are stray currents of energy, usually, but not always, of a negative kind. When some great and powerful evil occurs on Earth — a massacre, an act of genocide, or some other terrifying evil — the psychic repercussions would disrupt any settled land, no matter how close it hews to its earthly archetype, and so the waves of unfulfilled destiny manifest in Ainerêve as a vengeance-seeking beast. The fearful, hateful, angry, primitive core of its mind is a collective creation of those Earth-bound people whose horrifying death created the dragon — their anxiety, grief, fury, and despair. Normally dragons are drawn to travel the unsettled lands until they reach the borders of a settled realm, one with a culture the dragon’s subconscious collective memory recalls. Often this initial manifestation takes the form of an egg; I am told some dragons breed true, and thus, if two meet, they can create more eggs, a ripple of destruction that has crippled kingdoms, laid low empires, and killed more adventurers than legends can count. Abilities and Powers: We will speak first of typical dragons if any can be called such; a plume of fiery breath, hot enough to sear the flesh from bone; if airborne, a deadly swoop, snatch, lift and drop; hovering, blindness caused by whirled up soil and debris, plus strikes from every claw; a lashing tail that can knock even an ogre prone. Yes, those but recently hatched are easier to destroy, but where you see whelps, be ready for their larger kin. The dragon is sharp in all its senses, with the eyes of a hawk; although prone to slumber, they awaken easily. I have read of great invincible beasts that, sleeping for centuries, grow so vast that the awakening of such leads to cultural nullification and extinction in surrounding realms — if the beast is evil, and most are. A dragon’s breath is magic energy, made of fire, cold, lightning, or other destructive forms. Whatever the type of breath weapon, the dragon is immune to that type of damage. (Dragons increase in size and power with age, in 6 stages: whelp, young, adult, old, elder, ancient / TD, d10, d12, d14, d16, d18, d20 / EA d4, d6, d8, d10, d12, d14 / HP ×1.5, 2, 2.5, 3, 3.5, 4.) Caches of weapons, antique armor, masses of gold and silver, statuettes and gems, not to mention antiques, librams, and magics rare, will all be found in the abode of a long-lived dragon (a typical dragon’s hoard: 10,000–60,000 coins and 50% chance of 8 magic items). Abilities and Powers: Standard Shee possess the ability to shape-shift, allowing them to assume various forms for deception, concealment, or combat. Their glamor magic is powerful enough to manipulate perceptions, crafting illusions that can confuse or mesmerize their enemies. Proficient in Nature, Life, Illusion, and Plant magic, Standard Shee are formidable defenders of their territories, summoning the forces of nature or obscuring their enemies’ senses with ease. Weapons, Armor, and Treasure (WAT): Standard Shee often wield weapons made from natural materials, such as bows crafted from enchanted trees or swords forged from moonlight silver. Their armor, if worn, is typically light and made from leaves or bark, enchanted to provide protection without hindering movement. Their treasures are hidden deep within their groves or arboreal palaces and consist of rare gems, plants, or “nature-inspired crafts” worth between 500 to 1500 coins, reflecting their higher status compared to their minor counterparts. Shee Lord Type: Exceptional (Fay Nobility) Threat Dice: Arcane 3d12 ~ Melee 2d8 ~ Natural 2d6 Hit Points: 72 (48/24) [fast, large, preternatural; ×2.5] Damage Reduction: By any armor worn Saving Throw: d12 Battle Phase: d12 Notes: Shee Lords are formidable faerie nobility, possessing potent glamor and command over faerie magic. Legends and Lore: The Shee Lords are the aristocracy of the Fay, ruling with a captivating blend of grace and terrifying power. They embody the duality of the Seelie and Unseelie Courts, with some aligned with the light and others steeped in shadow. Revered and feared in equal measure, Shee Lords can be benevolent protectors or ruthless adversaries, depending on their whims. Their unearthly beauty is said to ensnare the minds of mortals, and their presence commands awe and trepidation. They are often found in their arboreal palaces, magical fortresses grown from the very heart of Fertile Glens, which serve as both their source of power and a reflection of their will.

Abilities and Powers: A Shee Lord is a master of glamor and illusion, capable of bending reality and perception with mere thought. Their magic extends beyond mere deception, allowing them to control natural forces, summon creatures of the wild, and reshape landscapes to their desires. In combat, they are deadly, wielding both spell and blade with unparalleled skill. Their glamor is not just an illusion but a manifestation of their power, capable of enchanting entire courts and bending even the strongest wills to their desires. The Price of Power: Though Shee Lords seem almost godlike in their domains, their power comes at a cost. Some whisper that their ties to the mortal realm weaken with each passing century, requiring offerings of beauty, artistry, or even life force to maintain their splendor. This dependence may fuel their capricious nature, driving them to acts of great generosity or terrible cruelty in an endless cycle of need and fulfillment. Mortals, such as the Eldritch Man, may find themselves in service to the Shee, their lives extended and powers enhanced in exchange for their loyalty and life force. Weapons, Armor, and Treasure (WAT): Shee Lords often wield enchanted weapons, heirlooms passed down through generations, each imbued with powerful magic. Their armor, if worn, is of the finest craftsmanship, often appearing as part of their glamor rather than physical protection. The treasures of a Shee Lord are vast and ancient, including relics of immense power, rare gems, and artifacts hidden deep within their arboreal palaces. These palaces, grown from the very trees they inhabit, are living structures, their halls winding through massive trunks and canopies, hidden from those uninvited. The wealth within is guarded not only by enchantments and traps but by the very forest itself, loyal to the Shee Lord’s will. Source of Evil Type: Legendary (avatar) Threat Dice: Arcane d20+30 ~ Melee d8+24 ~ Natural d6+18 ~ Ranged d6+12 Hit Points: 50 (25/25) [normal, supernatural, medium; ×2.5] Damage Reduction: Immune to non-magical weaponry Saving Throw: d10+10 Battle Phase Battle Phase 1 default or d20+10 for random initiative Legends and Lore: The Source of Evil, known by many names—Satan, the Accuser, Lucifer—was once a radiant angel. Even now, he can appear with an image of purity, though it is a cold, cynical purity filled with self-righteousness and pride. At other times, he takes on the bestial figure of folklore, horned and tailed: the Devil. Satan is wary of Baphomet, the usurper, and his consort Lilith, both of whom have turned their attention to Ainerêve. Initially, Satan viewed Baphomet’s interest in this dream realm with disdain, assuming the Sabbatic Goat sought only to rule in dreams. But a dread fear soon gripped Satan’s black heart—what if this realm of dreams was his true origin? Satan, once a persecutor of mankind, now fears that he is their creation, a miscarried spawn of their darkest fears and loathing. The possibility that Baphomet and Lilith seek to reshape Earth through Ainerêve’s dream realms fills Satan with a sense of dread. To counter this, Satan has reached out to Heaven, and together they have woven a web of energies across Ainerêve, a net of power meant to bind Baphomet. Both Heaven and Hell now watch with bated breath, waiting to see if they, too, are but dreams or mere facsimiles of a greater reality. Abilities and Powers: Satan’s powers are vast, bordering on omniscience and omnipotence—at least, so he believes, and perhaps he is right. When the Avatar of Evil manifests in the mortal realm, it often toys with humanity, presenting itself as a daring alternative to societal norms. But beneath the aristocratic facade lies a wrathful, hateful being, the complete antithesis of love. In combat, Satan is overconfident, often allowing vulnerabilities in his avatar form to taunt his attackers, as though mortal combat were merely a game. And to Satan, it is—a game in which he is the master.

Satan’s avatar regenerates 10 HP per battle phase and gains temporary hit points equal to the damage it inflicts on a target’s Passive Defense. Even if his avatar is slain, it regenerates from dust after four rounds, but only in Hell. Weapons, Armor, and Treasure: Satan’s avatar can wield any item, treasure, or device, including those of immense power. The treasure hoards associated with him are vast, potentially equivalent to a dragon’s hoard, valued between 10,000 and 60,000 coins, with a 100% chance of containing d8 magic items. Specter (or Limbo Spirit) Type: Standard (undead) Threat Dice: Natural d10 (+d8 non-physical harm via spectral touch). Hit Points: 25 (13/12) [normal, supernatural, medium; ×2.5] Damage Reduction: Immune to non-magical attacks and Energy Elementalism spells Saving Throw: d6 Battle Phase d10+12 Legends and Lore: Specters, ghosts, and spirits act as guides, allies giving clues to the living, wandering souls, or guardians of holy or unholy places.. Abilities and Powers: These ghosts seem almost solid, if not for a faint shimmering, but can turn invisible at will. Striking at one with non-magical weapons is pointless, as they are immune to such. They are also immune to Energy attacks since they exist as (spiritual) energy, the “fifth essence.” Thaumaturges can unravel a spirit’s manifestation with non-physical harm spells, especially entropic spells, which inflict double damage. The weapons of a limbo spirit appear real but are actually dense energy fields. Their weapons can be used to parry, but ghosts don’t often use such tactics. All damage from their attacks bypasses non-magical armor. Spectral touch: The spirit’s extra non-physical damage die, whether delivered by touch or weapon, is preternatural and can be blocked by armor — but only roll the extra damage die if the base Natural threat roll exceeds the target’s chosen active defense. Some specters have extra powers consistent with their nature. Weapons, Armor, and Treasure: These spirits don’t carry real equipment or treasure. Swipes (Astral Footpads) Type: Minor (extraplanar) Threat Dice: Ranged d10 (+d8 if using “kestros”) ~ Natural d4+2 (claws) ~ Melee d4 Extra Attacks: Astral snap d6+d4 (by touch) Hit Points: 20 (10/10) [normal, preternatural, medium; ×2] Damage Reduction: d4 astral field (phasing in and out) Saving Throw: d10; immune to teleportation and summoning Battle Phase d12+4 Legends and Lore: Small and fleet of foot, swipes look like small, fuzzy monkeys with huge eyes. As a species, they are mostly thieves with the most annoying hideout possible: the astral plane. They can shift at will into unsettled territory. These “astral footpads” are capable of traveling great distances, teleporting upon mental command. A swipe will steal a coin purse, and then with a brief shimmering and a “pop” sound, it’s gone. They are as intelligent as humans and organized into tribal, nomadic societies that invade small towns, quickly depleting food sources and other items, then moving on. Abilities and Powers: A swipe gets +4 to initiative to increase Battle Phase or random initiative roll (see Chapter 3, Combat). They use small weapons only and are expert dart throwers (using a “kestros” sling). Swipes can see in total darkness. Their teleportation ability may be used once per round for short distances and as a natural defense (d4 damage reduction), but if the swipe shifts into the astral plane for longer than a battle phase, it cannot return for d2 rounds (it must get reoriented). They speak the common language, which allows them to converse with travelers of all sorts, and are easily bribed. Unbeknownst to most, the swipes are experts at navigating the chaos of unsettled lands. Once a place is described to them, they can guide anybody there in a short amount of time. Astral snap: Once per round, a swipe can touch a creature and send its spirit into the astral plane, but only for a split second. The victim’s spirit snaps back into the body with a jarring, disorienting effect (d6+d4 stun effect vs. Fortitude → Willpower → Resistance). Weapons, Armor, and Treasure: Swipes are strange creatures, as their bodies exist almost in two places at once: the astral and the settled realms. They have some natural resistance to normal forms of damage and get a d4 damage reduction against every physical attack. They are immune to teleportation and summoning spells if they are unwilling to be affected by them (though they can choose to be summoned, hitching a ride to adventure, no conjurer has figured out how to control or command one). If found traveling alone, a swipe may only have a trinket or two (1–6 coins and 1% chance of 1 minor magic item), but if adventurers invade a swipe community in the astral plane, they will have a small trove: 100–600 coins and 20% chance of d2 magic items. Troll Type: Standard Threat Dice: Natural 2d12 ~ Melee 2d8 ~ Ranged 2d8 ~ Arcane 2d8 Hit Points: 60 (30/30) [normal, magical, huge; ×2.5] Damage Reduction: +2 / d4 Saving Throw: 2d4 Battle Phase d10 Legends and Lore: A hulking creature with black pitiless eyes over a huge angular nose, and small ears, a troll is a creature with ancestors born from the primordial earth, and is attuned to natural stone. Abilities and Powers: They can create effects mirroring those of earth elementalists. Powers (as the spells) are Minor Earthquake, Fissure, Earth Merge, Earth Strike. Weapons, Armor, and Treasure: Trolls can use large weapons, but usually prefer to rend and tear with bestial strength. An individual troll may have incidental treasure, such as a magical arrow used as a toothpick, but a troll lair will usually contain a treasure trove: 200–2000 coins and 30% chance of d3 magic items. Vampire Type: Standard (undead) Threat Dice: Arcane 2d12 ~ Melee d10+d6 ~ Ranged d4 ~ Natural d16+2 Extra Attacks: Vampiric bite or hypnotic gaze Hit Points: 60 (30/30) [normal, supernatural, medium; ×2.5] Damage Reduction: By armor worn and shield wielded if any Saving Throw: 2d12 Battle Phase d12 Legends and Lore: Several types of this horrid undead parasite exist, from zombie-like corpse-things shuffling in search of blood to highly magical specimens able to maintain a semblance of humanity — sometimes with an enticing charisma. Thus one might encounter a seductress, fanged and sultry, reminiscent of Lilith’s brood, with attendant vampiric horrors that are obviously dead: things risen but recently from the grave. Some vampires choose to revere Satan, some Baphomet, and not a few regard Lilith as their creatrix. Many display a self-assured disdain for all, as they term them, so-called and self-proclaimed deities. Abilities and Powers: In Ainerêve, where every folktale becomes a reality, a realm’s cultural base will dictate the vampire’s powers and vulnerabilities. Typical are powers of charm that can fascinate and disarm a foe, the ability to fly, either in human or bat form, shape-changing to bat or wolf. Some can take on the form of a mist and flow ’neath doors or through cracks; all can drink blood, regenerating and thriving upon it. Some can only enter an abode if invited, are repelled by garlic or holy symbols, burn in the sun, expire beneath the water, and die only if staked or perhaps beheaded. Vampiric bite: The vampire rolls a d16 versus the target's Strength specialty in Prowess to grapple, and upon success, bites the victim, which has a stunning effect (no Challenge Die for the vampire). Once grappled, stunned or not, the victim takes d6 blood loss per round directly to passive defense hit points. Although stunning is a physical affliction, the save to break the effect (versus the vampire's original grapple roll) is Willpower (Fortitude), allowed initially, and once per round. Once stunning is eliminated, an opposed roll of Fortitude with Strength may let the victim break free. Hypnotic gaze: If the vampire concentrates and gazes into its victim’s eyes, it can create a mind control effect (zero Challenge Die; the vampire rolls 2d12 versus the target’s Willpower). Weapons, Armor, and Treasure: Grave goods for some, for others all the finery of an aristocrat — much depends upon the vampire’s goals, its mode of operating, and its need, or not, to acquire material power (treasure is typically a trove: 200–2000 coins and 30% chance of d3 magic items). Werewolf Type: Exceptional Threat Dice: Natural 3d8+8 (bite, claw, punch) ~ Melee d10+8 ~Ranged d6 Hit Points: 80 (40/40) [normal, medium, magical; ×2] Damage Reduction: Immune to normal weapons except magic and silver Saving Throw: 2d4 Battle Phase d8 human or d12 wolf Legends and Lore: As with the vampire, to which it bears a degree of kinship, the werewolf is shaped by Ainerêve’s dream-reality. In some realms, it is undead; in others, an accursed human who has donned, willingly or not, a cruelly enchanted belt that forces wolf-form upon its wearers. Some werewolves appear as ravenous man-wolves, a bipedal mixture of man and wolf; in others, the were-creature comes as an exemplary wolf — bigger, fiercer, swifter than its kin, but a wolf nonetheless. In most cases, it is a living thing, a human that becomes a blood-hungry hunter of the night at the time of the full moon. Abilities and Powers: It has all the wolf’s abilities, and its bite may spread lycanthropy. Fearsomely strong and agile, with a matchless ferocity, it may be vulnerable to silver and otherwise unharmed by weaponry. It will only use melee weapons in humanoid form. Weapons, Armor, and Treasure: Usually none, save that which it possesses naturally in human forms. In some realms, skinning the werewolf and donning its hide will allow the wearer himself to assume wolfish guise. Often, the werewolf drops all its gear when assuming wolf form — although some, in a half-man, half-wolf state, maintain possession of weapons, armor, and other accouterments (a small trove: 100–600 coins and 20% chance of D2 magic items). Woodshade Type: Exceptional Threat Dice: Melee 3d8 ~ Ranged 2d4 ~ Power 2d6 Hit Points: 102 (77/26) (fast, medium, magical; ×1.5) Damage Reduction: 2d4 (Woodshade armor) Saving Throw: 2d4 +2 Battle Phase d10 Legends and Lore: Prince Mathin the Gray, the Sixth of the original Credran of Echoes Forest, had formed a pact with an evil fiend called Lord Savinoth, a demon of a realm of shadow, turning himself and eleven other Credran Elves who followed him into dark elves — woodshades — with powers related to shadow and night. The average woodshade has an ebony complexion, pale violet eyes, and raven-black hair. With their new powers as minions of the shadow lord, they became the wardens of Umbra Keep. Woodshades are tall, averaging over six feet in height, and the females are near or at six feet in height. Otherwise, they are as slender and lithely built as elves. Abilities and Powers: A woodshade can use the power Form of Night, as the spell, at will. He can also use the following powers three times per day, requiring no casting roll and incurring no maintenance penalType: Manipulate Shadow, Continuous Shadow, Shadow Shield. Shadow Shield: Solidifies shadow to create a dark shield (or augment an existing shield). Adds a shield damage reduction effect of +2 while maintained. Weapons, Armor, and Treasure: Most woodshades wear woodshade armor, which is magical dark leaf-patterned leather (2d4 damage reduction). Exposure to sunlight disenchants it. Each woodshade has the equivalent of a cache: $20–200 and a 10% chance of 1 magic item.
