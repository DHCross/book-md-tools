**The Ravenous Coast**

_The Ravenous Coast is a perilous stretch of land where reality bends and fractures under the strain of ancient powers. Beneath its ever-changing landscape, something stirs—a presence as old as the land itself. Chaos storms lash the region with increasing frequency and ferocity, fueling whispers that the borders between reality and the Unstable Wilds are crumbling. Stone-paved roads wind through hills scattered with wild olive groves and gnarled pines, their roots gripping sun-baked earth. Golden plains unfurl between jagged sierras, where rivers carve deep gorges, their paths shifting with the seasons. Caravansaries—weathered sanctuaries for travelers—stand along these routes, their thick walls marked by more than time. The air carries citrus and rosemary, but this is no tranquil land. The coast teems with unpredictable energies, warping both the land and its denizens. At the heart of this instability lies Aiti Muir’hahore, the dread Mother of the Murder Horde, whose imprisonment only amplifies the chaos. Unchecked horrors spawned from her influence roam freely: scalding acid rain burns the earth, sentient whirlwinds stalk the unsuspecting, and gaping maws devour the unwary. These relentless anomalies are grim reminders of the fragile balance between order and oblivion._

_What was once dismissed as a child’s tale now carries ominous weight, drawing her gaze ever closer to the light—or perhaps to something darker still, a force beyond comprehension or control._

**The Land's Second Chance: Bastion of Order**

Amid the relentless chaos of the Ravenous Coast rises Second Chance Keep, a bastion of civilization perched atop a towering promontory. This elevated plateau commands the surrounding landscape, its precipitous cliff face plunging 250 feet into the churning waters of the Ondevka River below. The cliff’s sheer drop forms a natural fortress, making direct assaults from the lowlands nearly impossible. The landmass itself has been carefully crafted through magic and the power of an undead workforce, sculpted into an incredibly defensible city with the Keep at its apex—a citadel of order defying the untamed forces of chaos.

The plateau’s edges are further fortified with high stone walls and strategically positioned guard towers garrisoned by resolute troops, their watchful eyes scanning the chaotic expanse for threats. This plateau upon which the city sits seems shaped by ancient, otherworldly forces, as though carved with the purpose of providing sanctuary in a hostile world. To safeguard its inner sanctum, particularly the affluent First Ward, razor wire spans the skies, a lethal barrier against flying intruders. The Keep's structured wards reflect its ironclad hierarchy, with each layer of fortification reinforcing a society built on control and security.

At the heart of this defiant stronghold stands the Quadlig, a 25-foot-tall monument with four massive tentacles that wrap around each other, grasping at the heavens. It is dedicated to Dovin the Grey, a god seeking to bring order from chaos. Known as the Dimensional Anchor, this towering statue radiates stabilizing energy, carving out a twenty-mile radius of tranquility within the chaos. Here, swamps once ravaged by primal energies have been reshaped into fertile fields.

This adventure follows the events in **_A0 Caravans End_**, an adventure that is available for download at [Hoskbrew.com](https://Hoskbrew.com) at no cost. It is recommended in the strongest words known to man that you run that adventure first.

**Synopsis**

The group, having recently won through the Sewer Challenge, has gained favor with the City Watch. Specifically, Commander Tiber Sentious has need of some capable problem solvers, and after reviewing the Mender Glass of their exploits in the sewers, determines the player characters are just the group for the task. It will relieve him of a debt owed to Alef, and, should the group fail, it is nothing to him. If they survive, however, he will have garnered an effective team. That, and he really wants to know what that prick Alef is up to.

The Adventure begins with the group receiving a sealed invitation to join Commander Tiber Sentious at his Tower in the Commanderie for prandium (lunch). Included is a pass to enter the 1st Ward and the Commanderie (See Player Aide: Invitation).

Tiber knows nothing about the task Alef will offer the player characters, but he intends to find out. An Imp named Graz’axl has been a useful companion and aided Tiber’s rise to power. With his aid, Tiber will soon know everything he needs to.

Alef offers the group a chance for reward and reputation. A thief stole one of his current projects, something he calls the **Possibility Bottle.**This item is so much more than what he set out to create. It’s a volatile magical artifact capable of warping reality itself. Creating it has left Alef physically and mentally scarred, and he is desperate to recover it, motivated by mounting gambling debts, a blood price, and the artifact’s corrupting influence. Anxious to conceal the bottle’s true nature, Alef downplays the danger, emphasizing urgency while masking his growing apprehension.

The adventurers’ search leads them to **Milo Chance**, the thief who fled with the Possibility Bottle. Laden with a lead-lined box to secure the artifact, the party delves into the criminal underbelly of the Keep. Their investigation brings them into contact with many of the city's citizens. Still, to name but a few, key players are:

- Haukvisl (in disguise as Goffery Goldwilt, Chaos Theorist and antiquarian).
- A cunning street urchin named Two’s.
- The Fence Fenrick Bruss, a shrewd operator entrenched in the city’s black market: he brokered the deal, hiring Milo to steal the item, bring it to the Black Rhino, and hand it off to Veck and the Broken Road Mercenary Company.
- The Crimson Coin Crew: a group of rough-and-tumble street thugs who, until recently, worked with Milo Chance to steal the bootle. They are bitter and angry, having been double-crossed by their former leader.

The clues eventually take the party to De'Castas Inn in the Rat’s Nest, a district outside the city walls. In the dimly lit confines of Milo’s abandoned room, they discover that Milo has fled the Keep entirely, setting his sights on the infamous Hel's Gate by way of the Black Rhino. With the trail cold and time slipping away, the party must prepare to leave the relative safety of the City of Second Chance behind.

**In Act II**, the adventurers journey along the **Ancient Path** to the **Black Rhino**. They face environmental hazards and dangerous enemies, including chaos-tainted creatures and chaos storms. The adventurers must manage dwindling resources and find shelter while balancing risks and rewards. They will face challenges like an out-of-control wagon, a Hungriger Ghul, a devastated caravan with a trapped girl, and an ambush from the deadly Abattoir.

**Act III** finds the adventurers at the Black Rhino, a fortified caravanserai and magnet for the region’s shadowy dealings owned by **Gnaeus Draust**, a pragmatic half-ogre. Here, a collision of forces unfolds: remnants of the **Crimson Coin Crew**, agents of the Broken Road Mercenary Company commanded by **Veck**, and the enigmatic **Haukvisl**—whose interest in the bottle hints at an even more significant agenda. The Possibility Bottle’s activation plunges all involved into an extradimensional nightmare, a pocket realm brimming with chaos energy. Within this warped space, the adventurers must battle unimaginable horrors and contend with the bottle’s corrupting influence.

**In Act IV,** the narrative culminates in a dramatic confrontation with the Broken Road Mercenaries, led by Veck. The adventurers' choices throughout their journey directly shape the final battle's stakes. They must defend the Black Rhino from Veck's relentless siege, utilizing their resources and alliances to repel the attackers and safeguard the Possibility Bottle. If they choose to flee, they risk leaving the Black Rhino and the bottle to Veck's forces, potentially causing widespread chaos and destruction. If they surrender the bottle, they may avoid immediate conflict but risk placing a powerful artifact in the hands of a dangerous individual, with potentially devastating consequences for the region.

**Act V The Journey “Home”**sees the characters victorious, having defeated the Broken Road Mercenaries and safeguarded the bottle. Like the sign said, “There are no Second chances in the Wilds”. The road back is more hazardous than the way there. The Lycan Cult and the remnants of the Broken Road Mercenaries chase the player characters, and they face other hardships and dangers.

**The Truth and the Truth within the Truth:**

**Alef Brickhouse**created the “Possibility Bottle” quite by accident. While attempting to enchant an extra-dimensional space within a prepared vessel, a massive surge of chaotic potential energy slammed into and ripped through him. This raw potential of chaos mutated Alef. Having dealt with chaos theorist**Goffery Goldwilt**in the past, Alef consulted him about the bottle. Goldwilt (actually**Haukvisl** in disguise) recognized the bottle’s potential but gave Alef little concrete information, only warning him that it was potentially dangerous.

In the meantime, the **Cult of Lycan**’s vendetta against **Mender**, one of the Three of Second Chance, has forced them into overt action. Divinations revealed the bottle to the cult, and that they could use it to bypass Second Chance’s wards. The bottle was the answer to capturing Mender–Vengeance was close to hand! They contacted the**Broken Road Mercenary Group**, tasking **Veck**, the band's current leader of cutthroats and smugglers, with recovering the bottle.

Veck contacted **Fenrick Bruss**, a fence and fixer located in the Rats Nest of Second Chance Keep. Fenrick, in turn, hired Milo Chance and the Crimson Coin Crew to steal the Bottle. Milo befriended the luckless Alef Brickhouse and learned from the drunken dwarf’s own mouth where the bottle was secreted in his workshop. Milo stole Alef’s enchanted key (giving him direct entry), made off with the bottle and what workshop inventory he could, and skipped town. Unbeknownst to Milo, he was assisted by Haukvisl, who breached the wards Alef had placed upon his workshop so the theft could take place.

Milo is now heading to the Black Rhino, hoping to meet up with and join the Broken Road Mercenaries. Veck and the Broken Road Company are currently positioned at a ruined mill approximately 10-12 miles away, near the River Ondevka – a strategic location for their operations.

GM Note: Veck and the Broken Company will arrive at the Black Rhino concurrently with Milo himself and any player character pursuers, ensuring a dramatic convergence at that location in **Act IV**.

**The Three:**The ruling body of the Lands of Second Chance—Duke Tyvin, the Wizard Priest Mender, and Duke Starwanderer, the Necromancer. Each of these beings is directly involved in the day-to-day events of the Lands of Second Chance. They founded the city-state some quarter of a century ago.

**Duke Tyven** is of Olven descent and commands the military forces, especially those beyond the range of the Quadlig’s influence. In his final act to protect the world, Creaos sacrificed himself, offering the elves a choice: to join him or remain in the prime reality, merging their essence with native genetic traits. Those who chose the latter became the Olven-folk, bearing names and characteristics that reflect a profound connection to nature, often exhibiting traits associated with specific animals. **GM Notes:** Handling Combat and Consequences, Non-Lethal Damage & Player Choices

Encourage players to consider non-lethal takedowns, especially when dealing with commoners, hired thugs, or law enforcement. This preserves moral complexity, allowing them to incapacitate rather than kill unless their characters actively embrace a darker path. Reinforce this option with mechanical clarity—let them declare attacks as non-lethal and describe subdued blows, chokeholds, or strikes meant to disable rather than slay.

**House Rule: Death & Negative Hit Points**

NPCs reaching zero hit points do not die immediately. Instead, they fall unconscious and only die if their negative HP total equals their Constitution score. This rule keeps combat flexible, letting players stabilize or spare foes if they choose, creating meaningful decisions beyond “kill or be killed.”

**Setting Fights in the Areas Beyond the Walls of the City (Rat’s Nest, Eastern Docks, etc.)**

Most combat unfolds in back alleys, abandoned courtyards, or dimly lit taverns—places where law and order feel absent. Beyond the walls, guards seldom intervene unless bribed, blackmailed, or directly involved. Figures like Tiber influence this lawlessness, instructing enforcers to look the other way when it suits them.

**Avoiding Legal Trouble & Narrative Consequences**

If the PCs cause trouble, hint that their actions do not go unnoticed. Graz’axl the imp discreetly observes and reports to Tiber, ensuring that reckless actions have consequences. The imp serves as a narrative tool—Tiber might send a warning, offer a way out, or leverage their mistakes against them. This keeps the law’s reach ever-present without stifling player agency and ensures that combat remains engaging, morally nuanced, and tied to the setting’s broader dynamics.

**Strange sights and practices:**

There are many unusual sights and customs in the city. One of the most important is the Mender Tubes that bring water into nearly all the dwellings in the lands of Second Chance, while other tubes carry away bodily waste. They were conceived along with other initiatives, such as Hoskbrew, to increase the overall health of the citizenry. A healthy citizen is a productive citizen.

One problem with this is that anyone caught throwing night soil and the like into the street is subject to summary execution. Those caught bearing false witness are executed for this crime as well. Unless spotted by official representatives, all such cases are subject to magical inquiry before execution. Never fear, however; the corpse will be put to good use by one of the many undead skeletal work crews that keep the city clean and in repair. It is said that these undead remember nothing of their past but have sapience just like anyone else. They are not evil, as the energy giving them volition is not negative but something alien. The Great **Quadlig** that grants stability to the Lands of Second Chance keeps the skeletal work crews on task. In their off time, the skeletons are often found playing at one game of chance or another.

**The Ancient Path:**  This roadway and others like it cover the whole of creation on Ar’Neth. The roads are self-healing and repair themselves over time. They are imbued with the magic of Order as enchanted by the god Creaos in the time before. Creatures that have succumbed to the “Chaos Spiral” are those so filled with chaotic potential that they are literally changing and morphing continually and will eventually unravel completely. Should one of these abominations attempt to step upon a section of the ancient path, they will explode. All of the ever-changing cells in their body lock up in an instant, and the pressure causes the breast to explode.

# Act 1: Beneath a Razor Sky

**Setting the Stage**

**Date: The 5th of Vend in the 1284 since Queensfall**  


Several days after the tumultuous events of _AO: Caravans End_, the player characters receive a discreet invitation from **Commander Tiber Sentious** regarding a possible opportunity. **Tiber**, a seasoned hobgoblin legionnaire known for his pragmatic approach, recognizes potential where others see raw inexperience and offers them an opportunity to make their mark in Second Chance Keep—a bastion of civilization set amid the untamed expanse of the Ravenous Coast.

**GM Note:** Most, if not all, locations within the city are covered in the **Ravenous Coast Setting book**.

**Entering Second Chance Keep**
 The PCs arrive at Second Chance Keep, a fortress city and a bastion of civilization amid the chaotic Ravenous Coast. This city, perched atop a towering promontory, is designed for defense, regulating movement and reinforcing security. Divided into five distinct wards, each serving a different social stratum, the Keep reflects a rigid social hierarchy guided by the 'Law of the Three,' which prioritizes order above all else:

- **First Ward: The Affluent Heart**
 Near the southern entrance and the Shores, the First Ward houses the elite. Polished cobblestone avenues lead past high-end shops and temples of white marble and black obsidian. Guard barracks and **Commander Tiber Sentious**’s own tower dominate this district, patrolled by hobgoblin enforcers. Razor wire stretches across battlements to deter aerial threats, and by day, banners ripple in the breeze; at night, enchanted lamps bathe the streets in a steady glow. Within the ward lie the sprawling mansions of the wealthiest citizens, accessible only by wizard-marked pass. At its heart, the Quadlig Monument stands in silent testament to the city's endurance.

- **Second Ward: Wealth & Vice**
 West of the First Ward, the Second Ward is where affluence blends with pragmatism. Grand residences stand alongside prestigious workshops and gathering halls. Here, the **Temple of Asmodeus**—with its black stone façade draped in crimson banners—casts long, foreboding shadows over the district, while the **House of Vice**, an elite gambling den and brothel guarded by barbed devils, caters to merchants, artisans, and ambitious schemers. At night, patrols pass through, though the watchful eye is less rigid than in the First Ward.
- **Third Ward: Merchants & Warehouses**
 The 3rd Ward is the largest of the five wards. It was once a massive refugee camp back inthe early days of Second Chance. Some of the original layout remains, though most was renovated and restructured by Mender and the undead work crews. The entrance to this ward is heavily fortified, as it was once the outer ring of the city. Some areas have quite narrow streets, as Mender set aside a good portion for gnomish enclaves. Those who dwell here often have small shops or work as skilled labor in the Fourth Ward.
- **Fourth Ward: The Industrial Core** Here, the city’s forges, workshops, and factories churn endlessly, filling the air with the clang of metal and the hiss of steam. The precisely cut streets are reinforced to withstand the endless flow of ore-laden carts, industrial machinery, and heavy-footed workers. Narrow alleys thread between densely packed homes and sweat-soaked factories, and towering forge stacks loom overhead, exhaling coils of thick smoke into the sky.


- **Fifth Ward: The Exclusive Enclave**
 Secluded in the southwest corner, the Fifth Ward is an exclusive enclave of estates and gated manors. Bordered by the city's outer wall to the west and south, it is home to the wealthiest elite who value privacy and space above all else. Access is strictly controlled, limited to residents, their servants, and invited guests. Wide, paved streets lined with elaborate fountains and manicured gardens testify to the residents' wealth and influence. Stone walls and iron gates shield this district, and hidden paths wind through the gardens toward the docks—venues for private dealings away from prying eyes.


**A City of Order and Intrigue**
Second Chance Keep is more than a fortress—it is a city built on rigid order, concealed vice, and a strict social hierarchy. Its defenses are as unyielding as its laws, with heavily guarded gates where tolls are strictly enforced. Those who fail to pay risk detainment, confiscation of goods, forced labor, or imprisonment—a constant reminder of the city’s ruthless rule. The ward system serves not just as a defensive measure but also as a means of control, restricting movement and cementing the divisions between its people.

**The Mission Unfolds**
 Within this complex urban landscape, the PCs are drawn into a delicate investigation. **Commander Tiber Sentious** introduces them to **Alef Brickhouse**, an eccentric dwarven enchanter desperate to recover a stolen artifact—an enchanted item known as “Alef’s bottle.” What seems like a straightforward task quickly unravels into layers of hidden agendas, as **Alef Brickhouse** remains evasive about the true nature of the artifact, driven as much by desperation as by its value. For the adventurers, the mission represents both an opportunity and a risk—a chance to gain a reputation as capable troubleshooters while navigating the criminal underbelly of Second Chance Keep.

The investigation begins with the elusive **Milo Chance**, the thief responsible for the bottle's disappearance. With a lead-lined box to secure the bottle in hand, the party ventures into the less-policed quarters of the city. They encounter various figures who might have information—from a savvy operator like **Fenrick Bruss** in **Rat's Nest** to a curious merchant of oddities in the Second Ward Market. Through persuasion, intimidation, or careful observation, critical clues about **Milo Chance**’s activities in the city's shadowy corners slowly emerge.

**GM Considerations**
 The stakes for the PCs in recovering the Possibility Bottle (the artifact known as “Alef’s bottle”) are high. The basic monetary reward is 500 gold pieces each per PC, with **Alef Brickhouse** offering an additional 400 gold pieces worth of gear, a wagon, and an extra 100 gp for expenses. Failure not only means lost rewards but also jeopardizes future opportunities with **Commander Tiber Sentious**. Ultimately, the PCs’ goal is to gain a reputation as capable troubleshooters for the Keep.

**Scene 1.1: Meeting Watch Commander Tiber Sentious**
The PCs are first escorted by disciplined hobgoblin guards to the mustering grounds of Second Chance Keep, where they meet **Commander Tiber Sentious**. **Tiber** sees promise in them. He explains that opportunities abound for those willing to prove themselves and introduces the prospect of a mission that might change their fate. This meeting plants the first seed of hope—and mystery—in the PCs’ minds, even as they remain unaware of the deeper intrigue that lies ahead.

**Scene 1.2: The Errand for Alef Brickhouse**
Following their encounter with **Commander Tiber Sentious**, the PCs are escorted to his tower. There, they meet **Alef Brickhouse**, an eccentric dwarven enchanter with a touch of injury evident in his favoring of his right side. Anxious and desperate, **Alef Brickhouse** tasks the adventurers with retrieving a stolen artifact—an enchanted bottle known only as “Alef’s bottle.” With only vague clues about the thief and his likely haunts, **Alef Brickhouse** slides a small, heavy, lead-lined box across the table and promises an upfront payment along with additional rewards for successful recovery. Before any further details can be discussed, **Commander Tiber Sentious** departs, leaving the PCs with a freshly issued Writ of Authority that grants them the freedom to explore Second Chance Keep and gather information. Unbeknownst to the characters, this errand is the first step toward unraveling a far more complex mystery.

## Scene 1.3: The Keep and First Ward

Freshly tasked by Alef Brickhouse, the PCs are granted a brief window to explore Second Chance Keep and gather information. They can pursue leads related to Milo Chance, the Possibility Bottle, and Alef's past, interacting with various NPCs and locations within the Keep. This scene provides an opportunity for investigation, role playing, and skill-based challenges before they delve deeper into the less controlled districts of the city.

**Core Conflict & Stakes**

The PCs must gather information and resources to find the stolen item. Their choices will influence their preparedness and investigation. This tests their initiative and resourcefulness. By forging connections, uncovering clues, and understanding the stakes, they prepare for a successful pursuit. A misstep here could endanger their confrontation with Milo and his gang. However, solid preparation might be the key to recovering the stolen item and securing their future. 

#### The First Ward – Administrative & Commercial Center

As the administrative and commercial heart of Second Chance Keep, the First Ward exudes an atmosphere of affluence and precision. From Tiber's Tower’s northern position, the ward reveals itself—a meticulously ordered expanse perched high above the Ondevka River. Polished cobblestone avenues, etched with subtle runes, stretch out like veins from the tower’s district and are maintained in pristine condition by tireless skeletal crews. Dignified temples of white marble and dark obsidian—each proudly bearing the tentacled eye of Dovin—stand in sharp contrast to well-appointed shops. Windows display exotic jewelry, rare artifacts, and finely tailored clothing beneath brightly colored awnings that bear various merchant guild insignias. In the distance, the imposing Commanderie complex—the central Watch Headquarters under Watch Commander Tiber Sentious—looms nearer to the main gates.

#### Looking Around

Now, as the players take in their surroundings, note these details that invite further exploration:

- **Hobgoblin Patrols:** Enforcers in polished black and silver armor, adorned with ornate insignias, stride beneath crisp First Ward banners.
- **Market Sounds:** The air buzzes with merchants' calls in various languages, the clink of coins amid spirited haggling, and the rhythmic drill and clang of steel emanating from the Commanderie.
- **Sensory Tapestry:** Exotic spices, cured meats, sweet temple incense, and fine leather intermingle with a subtle metallic tang from busy craftsmanship.
- **Cobblestones and Wind:** Underfoot, cool, smooth cobblestones provide a steady tread, while a chill wind whispers through the ward, hinting at an underlying tension.
- **Defensive Details:** Overhead, razor wire glints ominously along battlements. At the market’s edge, the Quadlig Monument rises from interlocking stone blocks etched with ancient runes, adjacent to a weathered notice board displaying various postings—including a stark flyer featuring the Twisted Wolf.

## Scene 1.5-A: Third Ward – General Atmosphere 

**Overview:**

This scene establishes the atmosphere of the Third Ward—a working-class, utilitarian district that, while less refined than the inner wards, is meticulously maintained by undead labor and diligent city services. Characterized by densely packed, practical multi-level stone buildings, modest trade halls, and tenement houses, the Third Ward reflects a no-nonsense approach to urban life. Though hinting at its refugee camp origins and retaining an underworld edge, it is far from dilapidated, presenting as clean and orderly, if undeniably rough around the edges. The scene immerses players in a setting of gritty survival and hidden information networks, serving as a story hook for encounters with figures like Kael and the Rabble (Scene 1.5-B) and investigations in the Rat’s Nest and De'Castas (the flophouse, outside the main walls of the Keep in the Rat's Nest).

**Read Aloud** 

**Entrance to the Third Ward:**

_Leaving the polished avenues of the inner wards behind, you step onto well-kept, though utilitarian, cobblestones marking the Third Ward. Orderly facades yield to a dense sprawl of practical trade halls and simple tenement houses lining narrow, twisting avenues. Here, practicality reigns supreme; buildings of sturdy, multi-level stone prioritize efficiency over luxury, embodying the district’s working-class ethos. The voices of merchants, laborers, and traders blend with the steady hum of daily activity while ever-present law-keepers, their armor functional rather than ornate, patrol with quiet vigilance._

**Third Ward General Atmosphere:**

Deeper within the district, it becomes clear: while the Third Ward bears traces of its past as a refugee camp in older structures, it is well maintained. The narrow, bustling streets are notably clear of clutter and refuse, presenting a utilitarian aesthetic: neatly aligned building fronts, clean surfaces, and purposeful design focused on function. The air carries a complex bouquet of scents – earthy notes from nearby farmland, the River Ondevka's briny tang, sharp iron from active workshops, and woodsmoke from street-side cooking – subtly interwoven with the pervasive aromas of spilled ale and hard-working sweat. Life here pulses with raw, determined energy; voices, machinery, and countless footsteps create a vibrant urban symphony. 

The Nexus Market is a source of indirect clues. Other encounters, like the ambush by Kael and the Rabble, are just outside the city at the Eastern Docks (Scene 1.5-B), and deeper investigations are found within the Rat’s Nest and De'Casta bunkhouse. 

**Nexus Market/Market Square (Day):**

At the Third Ward's northern tip lies the bustling **Nexus Market**, a market square strategically positioned at the convergence of the Third, Second, and Fourth Ward; a hub where these districts meet. By day, sunlight floods crowded stalls where merchants energetically hawk their wares. Bright awnings cast dappled shadows over displays of fragrant spices, cured meats, and overflowing crates of fresh produce. The market thrums with the clinking of coins, the rhythmic scraping of skeletal laborers maintaining stalls and pathways, and a lively chorus of overlapping voices. 

**Nexus Market/Market Square (Night):**

As night descends, the Market Square transforms under the soft, strategically positioned glow of magical lights. The daytime bustle gracefully subsides, replaced by a more subdued, enigmatic energy. Many stalls remain open, their diverse wares subtly illuminated by flickering lanterns casting long, dancing shadows across the clean, well-kept cobblestones. The night air carries a faint, almost imperceptible sulfurous undertone, mingling with enticing aromas of late-night street food and the persistent murmur of hushed conversations – the marketplace subtly shifting into a nexus of secrets and clandestine exchanges.

**Looking Around** 

- **Smells**: Sweet spices, briny river air, sharp iron, and a base note of sweat and ale.
- **Sounds**: Blacksmith's hammer ringing, carts creaking on cobblestones, bartering voices, distant laughter.

The briny air and sharp tang of salt mingle with the acrid smoke from distant forges. Though remnants of once-rebuilt dockside structures and narrow streets hint at a former order, recent territorial tensions—and incursions by critters from the Chaos Lands—have left parts of the docks in partial disrepair. This contrast foreshadows the lurking danger that awaits beyond the city walls.

- **Nexus Market/Market Square:** The Nexus Market is packed with stalls and merchants offering a vast selection of goods. Stacked crates and cloth-covered tables display enchanted relics, rare spell components, and finely crafted magical arms and armor, their runes catching the light in flickering hues. Weaponsmiths hammer out tempered steel, while jewelers set precious stones into rings and amulets, some humming with latent power. Among the wares, vendors peddle temporary magical effects—pastes, salves, and alchemical infusions stored in glass vials or wax-sealed jars. The air is thick with the scent of oil, metal, and incense, mingling with the murmur of negotiation and the rhythmic clatter of trade.

## Goffery Goldwilt (Scene 1.5-A-1): Nexus Market

In this scene, the PCs encounter Goffery Goldwilt at his stall in the bustling Nexus Market. Alef mentioned him as a chaos theorist who sells knowledge and interesting magical trinkets. Nobody is aware that Goffery is actually Hawkvisil, whose true identity and motivations are concealed. **The Sinders Eye**

The Sinder’s Eye is a smooth, cool obsidian orb with shifting crimson veins beneath its polished surface. It radiates faint, unsettling whispers at the edge of perception. Prolonged contact erodes mental resilience, compelling holders toward darker impulses. When this occurs, the orb emits a signal that has the capability to attract undead creatures within a radius of approximately half a mile. This signal radiates outward from the life force that is near the orb, effectively centering on the living being's location. 

Torvel's reluctance to handle the orb, attributed to strange whispers and the mysterious arrival of a ghoul at his shop, could be linked to this undead-attracting property if the orb was near a living creature while in his possession. _"Look at us! Chaos spreads like poison through our streets, and no one lifts a hand to help—not the Keep, not the Legion, and certainly not you!"_

He turns, addressing the crowd. _"They promised us an order! They promised us safety! But look at our streets! Chaos spreads like a disease, and our pleas go unanswered! The Keep does nothing! The Legion is blind! And now these outsiders come poking their noses where they don’t belong!"_

Kael’s accusation lands like a spark to dry kindling. The crowd presses closer, armed with makeshift weapons, their desperation palpable. The tension hangs knife-thin—one wrong move could tip this encounter into bloodshed.

### The Letter: A Calculated Drop

If forced to flee or when sufficiently threatened, **Kael** vanishes into the docks, using a Potion of Invisibility to escape. In his wake, he leaves behind a single letter, sealed and addressed to **Alef Brickhouse**. It's clear that Kael himself did not write the letter but was given it as part of his instructions, positioning him as a pawn in a larger game aimed at influencing Alef Brickhouse.

**Letter Text:**"To Alef,


Your actions ripple outward, creating consequences beyond your control. This serves as a reminder: debts unpaid lead to inevitable conclusions.

— Signed with the letter A."*

### Skill Checks & Player Choices

The PCs must decide how to handle the encounter:

- **Diplomacy (DC 20):** Convince Kael to stand down by redirecting his grief toward a productive solution.
- **Bluff (DC 20):** Sow doubt about Alef’s involvement or redirect blame to an external force.
- **Intimidate (DC 18):** Scatter the crowd with a show of force—though this may create long-term resentment.

**Peaceful Resolution:**
If the PCs succeed at de-escalating the situation through Diplomacy (DC 20) or Bluff (DC 20), Kael and his followers retreat without violence. Though the immediate threat passes, Kael remains a volatile presence who may resurface later. The player characters recover the letter addressed to Alef Brickhouse, but gain no further insight into the broader forces manipulating events. They do not realize they are being watched or tracked by the House of Vice, whose surveillance persists independently of Kael's actions. The encounter ends with the sense that deeper currents are at play, though their sources remain hidden—for now.

**Violent Escalation:**
 Should diplomacy fail or be abandoned, Kael’s followers—some visibly touched by Chaos—lash out, and combat ensues. Once Kael recognizes the battle is lost, he attempts to flee using a Potion of Invisibility. In his hasty retreat, his worn bag tears open, dropping a few items: the letter addressed to Alef Brickhouse, a small rag doll, and Kael’s personal diary. If examined, the diary reveals his deteriorating mental state, grief over the loss of his family, and deep hatred for the so-called Mighty Three. Its final entry outlines his task: to deliver the letter to Alef's shop, a mission he undertakes with visible resentment toward the faceless employers who exploit his desperation. Though the House of Vice is not named directly, the PCs may suspect greater powers are using Kael as a pawn.

**GM Note**: Even if it's unlikely the player characters would willingly deliver the letter to Alef Brickhouse, it hints at a larger conspiracy beyond the immediate theft (Even undelivered, it is a valuable piece of the puzzle, revealing the broader forces at play).

### 1.6-B Fence Fenrick (Rat’s Nest)

## 1.7 De’Castas Bunk House

Through their investigation, the PCs may have uncovered that the Crimson Coin Crew operates out of De’Casta, a rundown tenement and seedy inn known as a haven for gamblers, mercenaries, petty criminals, and those looking to disappear. It’s possible to come across this lead in various ways—word from Fenrick Bruss, whispers in the Nexus Market, or general inquiries into the underworld of the Third Ward. Perhaps they followed rumors linking Milo Chance to the location, observed the Crimson Coin Crew gathering there, or received confirmation from a reluctant informant like Knuckles Nab (see below). 

Whether pursuing leads from the Watch Commanderie, investigating the Rat’s Nest, or simply looking for a likely hideout, De’Casta has emerged as a key location in their search for Milo and the stolen artifact. Once inside, they may seek out Barb, the no-nonsense owner, watch the crew for signs of anxiety, or attempt to search Milo’s room—where they might find a map hinting at his next destination beyond the Keep. The whole truth of the Possibility Bottle remains out of reach, but the choices made here will shape the path ahead.

**Read Aloud**: _De'Casta’s is a dimly lit, ramshackle structure that seems to sag under its own weight. Like most of the buildings in the Rat’s Nest, it was built with what was at hand. De’Castas stands on the outskirts of Second Chance, just beyond the Keep’s main walls, deep in the Rat’s Nest. Despite its rough state, the place thrives as a gathering spot for ne'er-do-wells and the neglected. If Milo Chance needed to disappear, this is where he might have gone.

Depending on the PC's approach, this scene allows for stealth, negotiation, or direct confrontation. However they got here, what happens next is up to them.

As the GM, you aim to set a tense and dangerous atmosphere. De'Castas is a haven for criminals, and the PCs are likely to be met with suspicion. Emphasize the contrast between the flophouse and the order of the Inner Keep. This is a place where the Keep's rules hold less sway, and the PCs will need to be careful how they proceed.

**Key NPCs:**

She’s not easily intimidated but can be reasoned with if the PCs prove their worth.

Easily provoked but fiercely loyal to Barb.

- **Crimson Coin Crew**: Members of Milo's gang are present, though Milo himself is already gone. They are recognizable by their unwashed appearance and rough demeanor. 
- **"Knuckles" Nab**: A lanky, nervous youth with a twitch, constantly looking over his shoulder. His sharp instincts make him good at spotting trouble (high Perception). If cornered, he might reveal Milo was planning to meet someone outside the Keep.

Once the PCs gain access to Milo's room, they can search for clues to his whereabouts. The room should reflect Milo's hasty departure and lack of organization.

**Branching Paths:**

- Stealth: PCs can try to gather information without being detected, using Stealth, Perception, and Sleight of Hand.
- Negotiation: PCs can attempt to negotiate with Barb or other NPCs, using Diplomacy, Deception, or Intimidation.
- Confrontation: PCs could force their way in, leading to a brawl. This is the riskiest approach, but might be necessary if other options fail. A chaotic melee might create opportunities to capture or interrogate a gang member.

"If the Players Are Stuck" Prompts:

- Adapting the Scene: -- Stealthy PCs: If the PCs are successfully stealthy, they might overhear conversations or observe interactions that reveal more clues. -- Diplomatic PCs: If the PCs are skilled negotiators, they might gain valuable information from Barb or even convince a gang member to betray Milo. -- Aggressive PCs: If the PCs are confrontational, be prepared for a fight. If they capture them, they must decide what to do with their prisoners.

It remains unclear whether he intends to meet a contact, seek refuge, or simply escape pursuit. **Potential Outcomes:**

The PCs’ actions at De’Castas will shape the investigation’s trajectory. To make the scene engaging, the GM should build tension with vivid descriptions of the flophouse’s shady patrons and chaotic atmosphere, contrasting it with the Inner Keep’s order. # New Rules

## Acid Rain

Any creature that begins its turn in an area of acid rain must make a Fortitude save (DC 12\) or take 1d4 acid damage from the rain. Any armor or clothing that fully covers the creature will protect from the rain, but acid rain also has corrosive properties that can affect items. Every minute that an item is exposed to rain, roll a d12:

* Non-magical metal weapons, armor, and objects are destroyed on a 4 or lower.  
* Uncommon items are destroyed on a 3 or lower.  
* Rare items are destroyed on a 1\.  
* Very Rare and Legendary items are unaffected.

Some flora and fauna grow resistant to acid rain. If any resistant plants are used to shield a creature from the rain, no tests are necessary, the shield survives. 

## “The Bite”

A cruel affliction festering in the poorest gutters and alleys of the city, *the bite* is named not for how it’s caught, but for how it feels. Victims scratch themselves bloody, claiming something “under the skin” is chewing its way free. It spreads easily among the unwashed and malnourished—especially children.

**Contracting the Disease.** Prolonged exposure to unsanitary conditions, close contact with an infected individual, or inhabiting a hovel where the disease is common forces a DC 11 Fortitude save for every night spent resting. Failure results in infection.

Any creature spending more than 1 hour in close quarters (within 10 feet) of an infected individual must succeed on a DC 11 Fortitude save or become infected themselves.

**Symptoms.** Symptoms begin 1d3 days after exposure, typically in the form of red, swollen welts that itch uncontrollably. Over time, these welts split and weep a foul-smelling yellow fluid.

**Effects.** While infected, the creature gains no benefit from natural healing such as resting. The creature receives a \-5 penalty to Stealth and Diplomacy checks due to visible sores and constant itching. At dawn each day, the infected creature must make a DC 11 Fortitude save or become fatigued. If already fatigued, the creature instead becomes exhausted. If already exhausted, the creature becomes energy drained, gaining a negative temporary level that lasts until the creature cures The Bite or dies.

**Recovery.** The disease can be cured by magical means (*remove disease*, etc.) or by succeeding on three consecutive DC 11 Fortitude saves made at the end of a long rest, provided the creature has access to clean water and shelter.

## Sewer Rot

*Disease (contact-based), virulent and persistent*

**Contracting the Disease.** A creature with an open wound (HP below maximum, or who has taken slashing/piercing/bludgeoning damage in the last hour) who comes into direct contact with contaminated sewer water, filth, or refuse must succeed on a DC 13 Fortitude save or contract Sewer Rot.

**Symptoms**. The first symptom of weariness sets in within d12+12 hours

**Effects.** Stage 1 Fatigue (Days 1–2): The infected creature becomes fatigued and cannot recover from the fatigued condition until the disease is cured. At dawn each day, the creature must make a DC 12 Fortitude save. On a failed save, the disease progresses to Stage 2\.

Stage 2: Rot and Necrosis (Days 3–5): The infected creature’s fatigue intensifies, doubling the Strength and Dexterity penalties to \-4. Additionally, hit point maximum is reduced by 1d4-1 each day the disease remains untreated. Each dawn, make another DC 15 Fortitude save. On a failed save, the disease progresses to Stage 2\.

Stage 3: Advanced Sewer Rot (Day 6+): The infected creature’s fatigue is replaced with exhaustion, and cannot recover from the exhausted condition until the disease is cured. Hit point maximum is reduced by 1d6 per day. If the creature’s hit point maximum drops to 0, it dies. It cannot benefit from healing spells of 2nd level or lower unless cured.

**Recovery.** Magical means of disease removal of CL lower than 5 are ineffective against Sewer Rot, halting the disease’s progression for 24 hours, but not curing it. Magical means of CL 5 or greater or a DC 15 heal action are required to cure Sewer Rot, and must be followed by 7 days of care and 2 successful saves from the infected creature to fully cure Sewer Rot. A creature that recovers naturally is immune to Sewer Rot for 1d6 months.
