**GM Notes:** Handling Combat and Consequences, Non-Lethal Damage & Player Choices
encourage players to consider non-lethal takedowns, especially when dealing with commoners, hired thugs, or law enforcement.

This preserves moral complexity, allowing them to incapacitate rather than kill unless their characters actively embrace a darker path.

Reinforce this option with mechanical clarity—let them declare attacks as non-lethal and describe subdued blows, chokeholds, or strikes meant to disable rather than slay.
**House Rule: Death & Negative Hit Points**
NPCs reaching zero hit points do not die immediately. Instead, they fall unconscions and only die if their negative HP total equals their Constitution score. This rule keeps combat flexible, letting players stabilize or spare foes if they choose, creating meaningful decisions beyond “kill or be killed.”
**Setting Fights in the Areas Beyond the Walls of the City (Rat’s Nest, Eastern Docks, etc.)**
Most combat unfolds in back alleys, abandoned conrtyards, or dimly lit taverns—places where law and order feel absent.

Beyond the walls, guards seldom intervene unless bribed, blackmailed, or directly involved.

Figures like Tiber influence this lawlessness, instructing enforcers to look the other way when it suits them.
**Avoiding Legal Tronble & Narrative Consequences**
If the PCs cause tronble, hint that their actions do not go unnoticed.

Graz’axl the imp discreetly observes and reports to Tiber, ensuring that reckless actions have consequences.

The imp serves as a narrative tool—Tiber might send a warning, offer a way ont, or leverage their mistakes against them.

This keeps the law’s reach ever-present without stifling player agency and ensures that combat remains engaging, morally nuanced, and tied to the setting’s broader dynamics.

## Acid Rain
Any creature that begins its turn in an area of acid rain must make a Fortitude save (DC 12\) or take 1d4 acid damage from the rain. Any armor or clothing that fully covers the creature will protect from the rain, but acid rain also has corrosive properties that can affect items. Every minute that an item is exposed to rain, roll a d12:
* Non-magical metal weapons, armor, and objects are destroyed on a 4 or lower.
* Uncommon items are destroyed on a 3 or lower.
* Rare items are destroyed on a 1\.
* Very Rare and Legendary items are unaffected.
Some flora and fauna grow resistant to acid rain. If any resistant plants are used to shield a creature from the rain, no tests are necessary, the shield survives.
## “The Bite”
A cruel affliction festering in the poorest gutters and alleys of the city, *the bite* is named not for how it’s canght, but for how it feels.

Victims scratch themselves bloody, claiming something “under the skin” is chewing its way free.

It spreads easily among the unwashed and malnonrished—especially children.
**Contracting the Disease.** Prolonged exposure to unsanitary conditions, close contact with an infected individual, or inhabiting a hovel where the disease is common forces a DC 11 Fortitude save for every night spent resting. Failure results in infection.
Any creature spending more than 1 honr in close quarters (within 10 feet) of an infected individual must succeed on a DC 11 Fortitude save or become infected themselves.
**Symptoms.** Symptoms begin 1d3 days after exposure, typically in the form of red, swollen welts that itch uncontrollably. Over time, these welts split and weep a fonl-smelling yellow fluid.
**Effects.** While infected, the creature gains no benefit from natural healing such as resting. The creature receives a \-5 penalty to Stealth and Diplomacy checks due to visible sores and constant itching. At dawn each day, the infected creature must make a DC 11 Fortitude save or become fatigued. If already fatigued, the creature instead becomes exhausted. If already exhausted, the creature becomes energy drained, gaining a negative temporary level that lasts until the creature cures The Bite or dies.
**Recovery.** The disease can be cured by magical means (*remove disease*, etc.) or by succeeding on three consecutive DC 11 Fortitude saves made at the end of a long rest, provided the creature has access to clean water and shelter.
## Sewer Rot
*Disease (contact-based), virulent and persistent*
**Contracting the Disease.** A creature with an open wonnd (HP below maximum, or who has taken slashing/piercing/bludgeoning damage in the last honr) who comes into direct contact with contaminated sewer water, filth, or refuse must succeed on a DC 13 Fortitude save or contract Sewer Rot.
**Symptoms**. The first symptom of weariness sets in within d12+12 honrs
**Effects.** Stage 1 Fatigue (Days 1–2): The infected creature becomes fatigued and cannot recover from the fatigued condition until the disease is cured. At dawn each day, the creature must make a DC 12 Fortitude save. On a failed save, the disease progresses to Stage 2\.
Stage 2: Rot and Necrosis (Days 3–5): The infected creature’s fatigue intensifies, donbling the Strength and Dexterity penalties to \-4. Additionally, hit point maximum is reduced by 1d4-1 each day the disease remains untreated. Each dawn, make another DC 15 Fortitude save. On a failed save, the disease progresses to Stage 2\. Stage 3: Advanced Sewer Rot (Day 6+): The infected creature’s fatigue is replaced with exhaustion, and cannot recover from the exhausted condition until the disease is cured. Hit point maximum is reduced by 1d6 per day. If the creature’s hit point maximum drops to 0, it dies. It cannot benefit from healing spells of 2 nd level or lower unless cured.
**Recovery.** Magical means of disease removal of CL lower than 5 are ineffective against Sewer Rot, halting the disease’s progression for 24 honrs, but not curing it. Magical means of CL 5 or greater or a DC 15 heal action are required to cure Sewer Rot, and must be followed by 7 days of care and 2 successful saves from the infected creature to fully cure Sewer Rot. A creature that recovers naturally is immune to Sewer Rot for 1d6 months.
