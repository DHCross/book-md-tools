**MONSTER & NPC STAT REFERENCES**

**Notes on Disposition:**

Disposition is the successor to alignment in C\&C. For any instance in which alignment is mentioned, replace it with disposition. If something is good aligned, for example, they are now good-disposed or disposed toward good, or good/chaos or whatever. 

Disposition is typically two parameters separated by a slash. Whichever comes before the slash is the more dominant. Thus you can have a character that is evil/chaos in addition to one that is chaos/evil. These are handled on a case-by-case basis, or by referencing Monsters & Treasure. Some creatures are purely one thing, and thus can have a disposition of good, evil, law, chaos, or neutral. Note that in all instances, disposition is written as a noun rather than an adjective. Expunge ‘chaotic’ or ‘lawful’ from your vocabulary when discussing disposition. It is chaos and law.

It is good practice to do a search of the whole document for all instance of aligned, alignment, align, lawful good, neutral good, chaotic good, lawful neutral, true neutral, chaotic neutral, lawful evil, neutral evil, and chaotic evil to make sure you catch them all.

**Notes on Hit Dice and Levels:**

Originally hit dice were used in equivalence to levels for monsters and NPCs. Post-reforge, hit dice is used only to refer explicitly to the physical dice. Thus a monk’s hit die is a d12, for example, meaning they roll a d12 for each level. A dragon’s hit die is also d12. 

In all other instances of hit die/dice, it should now refer to level. It is good practice to do a search of the whole document for all instances of hit die, hit dice, or HD to make sure you catch them all.

When discussing character or monster levels, superscript should be used. Thus, it is 1st level, not 1st level. Further, it should not be typed out fully. Don’t do ‘first level’. Additionally, all instances of character or monster level outside of italicized stat blocks should be bolded. In the end it should look like the following:

The orcs chieftain fights as a **5th level** monster.

**Notes on Magic Items:** 

The bonus for any magic item should go at the end. Thus, a *\+1 longsword* should in truth be a *longsword \+1*. Further, in all instances where a magic item is mentioned by name OUTSIDE an already italicized stat block, it should be italicized, including the numerical bonus. 

When encountering a magic item, check the magic item names document for its old name to identify its new name. If the magic item does not exist in M\&T or one of our other books, it should either be changed to match an existing item, or a name matching the conventions of that document should be applied. NO MAGIC ITEM CAN BE PRINTED WITHOUT SOME DEGREE OF MECHANICAL EXPLANATION. This crops up a lot in our older stuff especially.

**Notes on Spells:**

All spell should be italicized when mentioned by name. When encountering a spell, make reference to the spell names document to identify its new name. There are three columns in the new names table, the first being the original name, the second being temporary names that were used in the intermediary period when we were first starting this process, and the third are the finalized names. It should be exceedingly rare that you encounter any names in the middle column. They are included mostly for my benefit.

If you encounter a spell that does not exist in our game (a very common issue with hastily converted 3.X D\&D material), find a spell that matches it most closely, or include a mechanical explanation. If the spell is simple enough, you may get away with just explaining it right then and there, but that is typically discouraged. Far better to collate it and all other fake spells in an appendix at the back for new spells. Refer to the PHB for the new spell format. When doing so, ensure that the spell in question does not appear by that name in dungeons & dragons, or in the SRD. We make an exception for single word spell names like fly, bless, etc., as no company can lay claim to those names legally speaking.

**Notes on Abbreviated Stats:**

There are a few types of shorthand stat blocks we use in our adventures which offer essential information at a glance. Several types are detailed below.

**Additional details about primes:** If they have both mental and physical primes, you denote them as mental and physical, not physical and mental. If they have no primes, you say ‘They have no primary attributes.’

**Additional details about treasure:** Do not use abbreviations pp, gp, sp, cp, et al. Write the coinage out using the material. Thus gp is replaced with gold, sp with silver, ect. If multiple types of treasure are listed, all valued in gold, it can be helpful to specify that specific coinage is ‘gold in coin’ so as to avoid confusion.

Doing it this way, we should see the changes below.

*They also carry 10gp and 50gp in jewelry.*	  
Become  
*They also carry 10 gold in coin and 50 gold in jewelry.*

**Re: Half Elves:** You must specify which lineage is dominant. I have not found a good standard for where to cram this in, so do whatever idk.

**Optional, Weapon Damage:** If you wish to list out all of an NPC’s attacks with weapons including damage dice, you are free to do so, but this can quickly make stat blocks unwieldy and bloated. If you do so, do not list said weapons among their equipment. We know we have the weapons already.

**Other Junk:** All vision types are now two word. Dark vision, deep vision, twilight vision, dusk vision. Bash attacks are now referred to as bash attacks. Improved grab is now called crushing grasp. 

**Basic Monster:**

**Example** *(This creature’s vital stats are Level X(dX), HP X, AC X, disposition X/X. Their primary attributes are mental and physical. They attack with X for XdX damage. They carry X. Their special abilities are \[extremely broad explanation, only if essential\]. Refer to \[source book\] for more information.)*

Only use gendered pronouns for a monster if it is explicitly stated, e.g. an orc war priestess, a dryad (all female), or a satyr (all male).

If the monster does not exist in one of our existing monster books, and it is not detailed in a new monsters appendix where you might direct them for a detailed analysis, you will either need to include them in a new monster appendix (see M\&T for formatting). If the monster is not substantial enough to warrant such treatment, or if they are extraordinarily basic mechanically, it may be worth it to cram all the mechanics into the abbreviated block for sake of brevity. Use best judgement on a case-by-case basis.

**Multiple Basic Monsters:**

**Example**, Number *(These creatures’ vital stats are Level X(dX), HP X, AC X, disposition X/X. Their primary attributes are mental and physical. They attack with X for XdX damage. They carry X. Their special abilities are \[extremely broad explanation, only if essential\]. Refer to \[source book\] for more information.)*

**Classless NPC:**

**NPC Name** *(This \[race\]’s vital stats are Level X(dX), HP X, AC X, disposition X/X. \[His/her\] primary attributes are mental and physical. \[He/she\] wears \[armor\], a \[shield, if any\], a \[weapon, if any\], and \[such and such treasure\].)*

**Classed NPC:**

**NPC Name** *(This 1st level \[race\] \[class\]’s vital stats are HP X, AC X, disposition X/X. \[His/her\] primary attributes are strength, dexterity, and constitution. \[His/her\] significant attributes are \[any attribute over 12 or below 9\]. \[He/she\] wears \[armor\], a \[shield, if any\], a \[weapon, if any\], and \[such and such treasure\].)*

If they can cast spells, list their spells available per day, but do not list out their itemized spell list UNLESS it is absolutely essential to the function of an encounter. Spells go after equipment as follows:

*\[He/she\] can cast the following number of \[class\] spells each day: 0-X, 1st-X, 2nd-X, 3rd-X, 4th-X.)*

However many it is up to whichever level. Note that for spells, superscript is not used. It’s 1st, not 1st. Superscript is reserved only for when discussing character or monster levels.

**Order of attributes for primes and significant attributes:** There is a tendency to order attributes by most to least important for an NPC. This is a bad practice. They should be listed in the order they are explained in the PHB. Strength, dexterity, constitution, intelligence, wisdom, and charisma. 

Example:

**NPC Name** *(This 10th level human fighter’s vital stats are HP 50, AC 16, disposition neutral/good. His primary attributes are strength, constitution, and charisma. His significant attributes are strength 16, dexterity 14, wisdom 7, charisma 18\. He wears elven chain, a small wooden shield, a bastard sword, and 1,000,000 gold in coin.)*

**Multi/Dual Classed NPC:**

**NPC Name** *(This \[race\] 1st level \[class 1\]/1st level \[class 2\]’s vital stats are HP X, AC X, disposition X/X. \[His/her\] primary attributes are strength, dexterity, and constitution. \[His/her\] significant attributes are \[any attribute over 12 or below 9\]. \[He/she\] wears \[armor\], a \[shield, if any\], a \[weapon, if any\], and \[such and such treasure\].)*

Note that race goes before class here. This is purely because it flows better linguistically. 

**Concerning Full Monster Stat Blocks:**

New monsters in a new monster appendix must be updated to the current trade dress for our monsters. Refer to your up-to-date copy of M\&T for details. Spots that may catch you are sanity, biome, and climate, as these are not features in the older versions of the block.

**Sanity** is expressed as a value. Use the following table, and your best judgement on what you think a monster’s sanity score should be. Most monsters will not have a score, as they are not innately horrific. 

| SAN rating | Examples |
| :---- | :---- |
| 0/1 | Sudden, startling reveal – “Jump scare” |
| 0/1d4 | Something unexpectedly horrific: did that shadow just move on its own? Something is watching us, I know it\! Seeing a fantasy goblin on the streets in a modern city. |
| 0/1d6 | Coming across the scene of a horrific, but mundane, murder. |
| 1/1d4 | Stumbling across a situation that opens the door to the realization of occult truths. |
| 1/1d6 | Choosing to delve deeply into forbidden arcane truths, knowing what you are getting yourself into and still making the choice. |
| 1/1d8 | Minor demons, aberrations, and packs of undead; encountering a scene of multiple murders with clear occult or unnatural, bestial overtones. |
| 1/1d10 | A horde of zombies coming your way; catching an occult serial killer or mass murderer in the process. |
| 1/1d12 | Full exploitation/cult film carnage and death. Mass murder / death camps. |
| 1/2d6 | Ancient master vampires; the realization one is dealing with an obscenely powerful entity. |
| 2/2d6 | Seeing a Great Old One or ancient true demon lord in its most horrific full glory; realizing the apocalypse is right around the corner and you’re not sure there’s anything you can do to stop it. |

**Climate** is the temperature, moisture, and weather. The options available to you are Tropical, Sub-Tropical, Temperate, Semi-Arid, Arid, Sub-Arctic, and Arctic. You can fit multiple in at once by saying something like Any Non-Arctic, or Sub-Tropical to Semi-Arid, and other things along those lines.

**Biome** is the physical features of an environment. Commonplace terms are used. These are Forests (including jungles), Grasslands, Deserts, Freshwater (including Wetlands), Saltwater, Underground, and other extraplanar origins (Elemental Plane of Fire, et al.). If a creature can be found in any type of water, you would say Any Water, rather than list out Freshwater and Saltwater separately.
